One thread I'd be interested in developing further: the data foundations that AI
depends on appear to track clinical quality independently of AI itself. Snowdon
et al. (JMIR 2024;26:e56316, https://doi.org/10.2196/56316) found hospitals at
the top 2 EMR adoption levels had 3.25 times higher odds of better Leapfrog
Safety Grades. My framework argues that the organizational knowledge needed to
sustain that foundation is exactly what erodes through staff turnover, which has
direct implications for whether AI deployments hold up over time.

Three corrections were made to the initial draft before sending.

1. ATTRIBUTION. The draft presented the data-quality-to-outcomes result as a
   finding of the accepted paper. It is not. It comes from snowdon2024, cited at
   paper.md:128, and is credited by name in the sent version. What the paper
   contributes is the synthesis and the framework, not the odds ratio.

2. PRECISION OF THE CLAIM. The draft said institutions that "have cleaned their
   data" are "more likely to improve clinical outcomes." Snowdon et al. is a
   cross-sectional observational study measuring digital and EMR adoption
   maturity, not data cleanliness specifically, and reports odds of holding a
   better safety grade at one point in time, not improvement over time. Both the
   exposure variable and the temporal claim were corrected. The audience is
   physician-researchers, who would notice.

3. PUBLICATION STATUS AND LINK. The draft said "recently published." The article
   is accepted but held at final production pending the in-house visual abstract
   (agreed with Laura McReynolds 2026-08-11), so "accepted for publication" is
   what is accurate as of sending.

   The draft linked https://preprints.jmir.org/preprint/96541/accepted. Replaced
   with the DOI, https://doi.org/10.2196/96541, which was verified on 2026-08-21
   to resolve (302) to that same preprint page today and will silently begin
   resolving to the final article once the hold lifts. Same reasoning as the
   repo's Zenodo concept-DOI rule: cite the identifier that tracks the current
   version, never the version-pinned URL. An email sitting in an inbox for weeks
   is exactly the case that rule exists for.
